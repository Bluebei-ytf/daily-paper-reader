## 摘要
蛋白质功能由构象系综决定，构象系综可被视为分子构象上的高维概率分布。然而，这些分布的统计组织通常仅被隐式表示，或通过模拟轨迹的集合，或在高容量生成模型中。在此，我们介绍了PHASE（用于系综采样的蛋白质哈密顿量，Protein Hamiltonians for Sampling of Ensembles），这是一种系统特异性的框架，将原子级构象系综转化为明确且可解释的统计模型。将PHASE应用于来自腺苷A2A受体约37微秒原子模拟的十个构象系综时，仅包含6埃内局部残基耦合的哈密顿量便能够重现残基级和成对微态统计信息，包括模型中未直接耦合的残基之间的相关性。此外，独立拟合的非活性和活性参考哈密顿量定义了一个端点偏好坐标，该坐标将新采样的配体、效应子和构象依赖性系综沿A2A激活景观进行组织，而无需将这些生化标签作为模型输入。最后，一个簇条件化的全原子重建模型保留了新采样配置的既定残基微态模式，从而闭合了粗粒化-采样-反向映射循环。所产生的离散表示还支持直接QUBO编码，可实现经典退火，并为未来量子退火实施提供了途径。因此，PHASE提供了一种适用于蛋白质的通用方法，用于构建紧凑、可解释且原子层面可实现的蛋白质构象系综统计模型。

## Abstract
Protein function is governed by conformational ensembles, which can be viewed as high-dimensional probability distributions over molecular conformations. Yet the statistical organization of these distributions is often represented only implicitly, either through collections of simulation trajectories or within high-capacity generative models. Here, we introduce PHASE (Protein Hamiltonians for Sampling of Ensembles), a system-specific framework that converts atomistic conformational ensembles into an explicit and interpretable statistical model. Applied to ten conformational ensembles derived from approximately 37$μ$s of atomistic simulations of the adenosine A2A receptor, Hamiltonians containing only local residue couplings within 6$\mathring{A}$ reproduce residue-wise and pairwise microstate statistics, including correlations between residues that are not directly coupled in the model. Moreover, independently fitted inactive and active reference Hamiltonians define an endpoint preference coordinate that organizes newly sampled ligand-, effector- and conformation-dependent ensembles along the A2A activation landscape without receiving these biochemical labels as model inputs. Finally, a cluster-conditioned all-atom reconstruction model preserves the prescribed residue microstate patterns of newly sampled configurations, closing the coarse-graining-sampling-backmapping cycle. The resulting discrete representation additionally admits direct QUBO encoding, enabling classical annealing and providing a route toward future quantum-annealing implementations. PHASE therefore provides a protein-general procedure for constructing compact, interpretable and atomistically realizable statistical models of protein conformational ensembles.
